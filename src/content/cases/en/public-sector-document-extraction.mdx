## Context and problem

A public-sector organization uses an LLM pipeline in production to build a
legal knowledge base from long decisions. The pipeline extracts the parties'
arguments, extracts the reasoning behind each decision, and synthesizes the
final holding. An earlier expert review had found errors in most outputs.

The pipeline is sequential, so an early error contaminates every later step,
and the solution had to run on the client's own infrastructure with
open-weight models.

## What was done

<PipelineDiagram
  rotulo="Extraction pipeline, from segmentation to the knowledge base"
  etapas={['Segmentation', 'Extraction', 'Synthesis', 'Verification', 'Knowledge base']}
/>

- **Phase 1:** rebuilt the prompts with domain experts on a frontier model and
  measured the gain with a paired evaluation: the same documents, before and
  after, with a formal significance test.
- **Phase 2:** carried the improvement to the open-weight model used in
  production, with better document segmentation and automatic checks that
  flag suspicious outputs for human review.
- **Phase 3:** evaluated candidate models with prompts and documents held
  fixed, to separate the gain from the prompts from the gain from the model.

## Results

- Both differences are statistically significant (Wald test, p &lt; 0.001).
- On the open-weight production model (phase 2), the error rate per extracted
  ground fell from 75.0% to 48.6% on the same 99 documents.
- With prompts held fixed (phase 3), the gain from the prompts carried over
  to new models, and the best configuration brought documents with any error
  down from 73.1% to 25.3%.

## Lessons

- **Paired evaluation instead of two loose numbers:** comparing the same
  documents removes sample variance and allows a formal test.
- **Isolate variables:** fixing prompts and documents is the only way to know
  whether the model or the prompt caused the improvement.
- **The verifier flags, a human decides:** nothing is discarded
  automatically.
