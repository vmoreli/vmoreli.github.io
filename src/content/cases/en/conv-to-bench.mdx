## Context and problem

Reliable benchmarks depend on expensive curation. BigCodeBench, for example,
took about a year of work from 20 authors. Meanwhile, every company that puts
an LLM in production accumulates logs of real conversations. Those logs show
what users actually ask for and how they correct the model over the
conversation, but they rarely become evaluation.

- Existing automatic methods (such as Arena-Hard-Auto) use only the first
  turn, compare answers pairwise against a baseline, and depend on expensive
  proprietary models.
- For a company without a frontier-model budget, that rules out having its
  own continuous evaluation.

## What was built

<PipelineDiagram
  rotulo="Conv-to-Bench pipeline in three phases"

    'Synthesis: self-contained instruction + checklist',
    'Item-by-item evaluation and ranking',

A three-phase pipeline:

1. **Selection and filtering:** conversations from LMSYS-Chat-1M and WildChat
   grouped by topic (BERTopic) and filtered through a multi-stage funnel with
   adaptive sampling that balances diversity and quality. There were 16,731
   candidate dialogues.
2. **Synthesis:** for each conversation, an LLM consolidates the user's
   request, including the refinements made in later turns, into a
   self-contained instruction, paired with a checklist of binary, verifiable
   requirements.
3. **Evaluation and ranking:** LLM judges check each checklist item in each
   candidate model's answer. The final ranking is compared with reference
   rankings.

Statistical rigor: Spearman and Kendall correlations with bootstrap CIs,
Bradley-Terry, Pair Rank Brier Score, separability, Cohen's κ and
Krippendorff's α for inter-judge agreement, plus a verbosity-bias check.

## Results

### Workshop version (published)

- Spearman **ρ = 1.000** with BigCodeBench (an expert-written benchmark),
  against 0.943 for Arena-Hard-Auto with the same judge.
- LLM judge with **κ = 0.705** agreement with human-verified ground truth.
- No verbosity bias in the evaluators (low correlation between answer length
  and score).

Ablation: checking the checklist item by item beats pairwise comparison on
every metric: +0.146 Spearman, +22.8 pp separability, +31.7 pp agreement, and
−0.106 Brier.

## Why this matters outside academia

Any company with conversation logs can turn them into its own benchmark,
cheap and aligned with what its users actually ask for, and use it to choose
a model or validate product changes.
